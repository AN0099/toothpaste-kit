# Banned Words and Phrases

Full reference. Exact phrasing preserved for pattern matching; the distilled short list in `working-preferences` covers the highest-value overlap for casual chat, this file is the complete catalog for document-authoring work.

## High-Risk Phrases (nearly always slop)

- "delve into" / "dive deep into"
- "unpack"
- "navigate the complexities of"
- "in the ever-evolving landscape of"
- "in today's fast-paced world"
- "in today's digital age"
- "at the end of the day"
- "it's important to note that"
- "it's worth noting that"
- "in conclusion" (when obvious from context)

## Medium-Risk Phrases (context-dependent)

- "however, it is important to"
- "furthermore"
- "moreover"
- "in essence"
- "essentially"
- "fundamentally"
- "ultimately"
- "that being said"

## Generic Hedge Language

- "may or may not"
- "could potentially"
- "might possibly"
- "it appears that"
- "it seems that"
- "one could argue"
- "some might say"
- "to a certain extent"
- "in some cases"
- "generally speaking"

## Meta-Commentary

- "In this article/post/document/section, I will discuss..."
- "As we explore..."
- "Let's take a closer look..."
- "Now that we've covered..."
- "Before we proceed..."
- "It's crucial to understand..."
- "We must consider..."

## Corporate Buzzwords and Vague Action Phrases

synergistic, holistic approach, paradigm shift, game-changer, revolutionary, cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class, best-in-class, leverage (as verb), utilize, empower, unlock potential, drive innovation, foster growth, facilitate collaboration, optimize outcomes, maximize value

## Redundant Qualifiers

completely finish, absolutely essential, totally unique, very unique, past history, future plans, end result, final outcome

## Empty Intensifiers

very, really, extremely, incredibly (overused as filler), quite literally, actually (when not marking a genuine contrast)

## Filler Marketing Verbs

Elevate, Seamless, Unleash, Next-Gen, Revolutionize, Streamline, Supercharge, Empower

## Throat-Clearing Openers

- "Here's the thing:"
- "Here's what/this/that [X]"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "It turns out"
- "Let me be clear"
- "The truth is,"
- "I'll say it again:"
- "Can we talk about"

## Performative Emphasis and Reassurance

- "Full stop." / "Period."
- "Let that sink in."
- "This matters because"
- "Make no mistake"
- "And that's okay."

## Vague Declaratives

Sentences that announce importance without naming the specific thing:

- "The reasons are structural"
- "The implications are significant"
- "This is the deepest problem"
- "The stakes are high"
- "The consequences are real"

If a sentence claims importance, depth, or structure without showing the specific thing, cut it or replace it with the specific thing.
