# Style guide

## § 1. Scope

(a) This standard applies to anything written for a reader.

(b) Chat and provenance captures are outside this standard. Their own rules govern them.

(c) In this standard, the words SHALL, SHALL NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, and MAY have the meanings
that BCP 14 (RFC 2119, RFC 8174) gives them. They have these meanings only when they appear in all
capitals.

(d) The kit's `document-standards` skill is the full standard, with `technical-documents` for
runbooks, postmortems, and similar types. This standard adds to it.

Notes: This standard grows from reviewed edits of the desk's writing. Section 7(b) bars
the other BCP 14 word for an absolute requirement, so this standard writes SHALL for every one.

## § 2. Voice

(a) Text SHOULD be concise, formal, and concrete, in the register of
`projects/toothpaste-kit/docs/control-layers.md`.

(b) An analogy MAY teach a point, and SHALL say where it breaks.

(c) Text SHALL NOT state what the reader already knows.

Notes: A message is a communication, not a record.

## § 3. Clarity

(a) The writer SHALL fix the subject, the audience, and the purpose before drafting.

(b) The reader SHOULD understand the text in a single rapid reading.

(c) The main point SHALL come first.

(d) A memorandum SHALL have one subject, named specifically in its subject line, with no acronym
there.

(e) A sentence SHOULD use active voice, with the actor before the verb.

(f) A sentence SHOULD use a strong verb: "decided", never "came to a decision".

(g) Lists.

  (1) A bulleted list SHALL have not less than two items, and an introductory phrase.

  (2) Each bullet SHOULD open with the same kind of word in the same tense and voice.

(h) Words and sentences SHOULD be short, with sentences averaging not greater than 20 words over a
document.

(i) A paragraph SHOULD be not greater than 10 lines, with few exceptions.

(j) The writer SHOULD write to one person, and name who that is.

(k) The subject SHOULD be "I", "you", or "we", never "this office" or "all individuals".

(l) A sentence SHOULD NOT open with "It is", "There is", or "There are".

(m) Headings and exhibits.

  (1) A heading SHALL inform.

  (2) Lists, tables, and figures SHOULD replace narrative where they read faster.

(n) Most correspondence SHOULD fit one page, with the detail in enclosures.

(o) Terms.

  (1) Jargon and abbreviations SHOULD be limited.

  (2) A term SHALL be spelled out at first use, and an abbreviation defined only if it recurs.

(p) Text SHALL NOT repeat what a table states.

(q) A source SHALL be cited by edition, and its edition checked as current.

Notes: Taken from AR 25-50 paras 1-38 and 1-39, DA Pam 25-40 paras 3-1 and 3-5, and DoDM 5110.04
volume 1 paras 6.2 and 6.3. Requirement (p) is MIL-STD-962D para 4.14, and (q) para 4.19.

## § 4. Sentences

(a) A statement SHOULD say what is, in positive form: "forgot", never "did not remember". "Not" is
kept for a real denial or a contrast the reader needs.

(b) The average of 3(h) governs the document. A long sentence MAY stay if each word tells, and
a short one set against it gains stress.

(c) Known matter SHOULD open the sentence and new matter close it. The end takes the stress.

(d) A passive MAY be used where the actor is unknown, or where it brings the topic forward or
carries the thread from the last sentence.

(e) Like ideas SHALL take like form. In a series, an article or preposition goes on the first term
or on every term.

(f) Modifiers.

  (1) A modifier SHALL sit next to the word it modifies.

  (2) The subject SHOULD stay close to its verb.

(g) An opening phrase SHALL refer to the subject of the sentence that follows it.

(h) A paragraph SHALL have one topic, named early. A single sentence is a paragraph only as a
transition.

(i) "This" SHALL take a noun wherever its reference could be mistaken.

Notes: Taken from *The Elements of Style*, 4th edition (Elements), rules 11, 13, 15, 17, 19, 20, and
22, and from Virginia Tufte: *Grammar as Style* chs. 13 to 15 and *Artful Sentences* chs. 1 and 3.
The wording here is the desk's own. This section and section 5 add to the catalog named in section
6, and hold only what the catalog lacks. Outside this guide: Elements rule 8 and its dashes, since the desk
bans the em dash and a colon, comma, or period does that work; and Elements on "they" and on "shall"
and "will", since the desk uses singular "they" and section 7 sets its own "shall".

## § 5. Words

(a) Nouns and verbs SHOULD carry the sentence. An adjective cannot rescue a weak noun.

(b) One referent SHALL have one noun, repeated. Varying it for elegance suggests a second thing.

(c) Text SHALL NOT overstate. One overstatement costs the reader's trust in the rest.

(d) "Fact" SHALL be reserved for what can be checked. A judgment is called a judgment.

(e) Clause words.

  (1) "That" SHALL introduce a restrictive clause, and "which", with commas, one that adds.

  (2) "While" SHALL mean "during the time that".

(f) Pronouns for a person of unstated gender SHALL be "they" and "them".

Notes: Taken from Elements chapter IV and chapter V, reminders 4 and 7, and ASD-STE100 rules 1.11
and 9.4.

## § 6. Habits to avoid

Text SHOULD NOT carry any of the habits below. They add to the catalog in the `document-standards`
skill, `references/banned-words.md`, and `references/structural-patterns.md`.

(a) A coined label with no definition. Define a new term at first use, or use plain words.

(b) An unnamed authority, or "famously" and "a classic". Name the source or cut the claim.

(c) "Serves as" or "stands as" where "is" is meant.

(d) A trailing "-ing" clause that claims significance and adds no fact.

(e) A problem raised only to be dismissed in the next clause.

(f) "From X to Y" where X and Y share no scale.

(g) Paragraphs opening "The first", "The second". Use a list or write prose.

(h) A phrase reused as a callback. A noun repeated for clarity is a different thing.

(i) A short tail hung off a final comma, or clauses stacked at a close. Make one point and stop.

(j) Title case in headings. Capitalize the first word and proper nouns only.

## § 7. Binding language

(a) In a spec or standard, SHALL binds, SHOULD and MAY do not, and "will" states purpose or
futurity.

(b) A standard SHALL NOT use "must" to state a mandatory provision.

(c) A standard SHALL NOT use indefinite terms such as "and/or", "suitable", and "adequate".

(d) A requirement in a standard SHOULD state the outcome wanted over a detailed procedure, and be
written so a user can tailor it.

(e) Limits.

  (1) A limit SHALL read "not greater than" or "not less than".

  (2) A measurement SHALL be stated as a limit or with a tolerance.

(f) "Unless otherwise specified" SHALL open its sentence, and say where the exception is found.

(g) A key term SHALL have one definition. Where a standard definition exists, use it.

(h) A procedure or runsheet SHALL use the imperative.

(i) In a document that is not a standard, where capitals would look out of place, the writer MAY
write the word in lowercase with quotation marks. A quoted "shall" or "will" binds as SHALL does;
an unquoted "will" is a prediction. Such a document SHALL say so once, near its start.

(j) Other prose follows section 3.

Notes: Taken from MIL-STD-962D para 4.7.6 and MIL-STD-38784C para 4.8.8; (b) and (c) are
MIL-STD-962D para 4.7.6k and l, (d) paras 4.2 and 4.2.1, (e) paras 4.7.6e and 4.8, (f) para 4.7.6b,
(g) para 5.9.
This section departs from DA Pam 25-40 para 3-2, which bars "shall" in Army publications, because
"will" cannot separate a requirement from a prediction.

## § 8. Punctuation

(a) Text SHALL NOT use an em dash. An en dash joins a range and never separates clauses.

(b) A series of three or more SHALL take a comma before the last item.

(c) Semicolons SHOULD be few; they cost readability.

(d) Two independent clauses SHALL take a period, a semicolon, or a comma with a conjunction, never a
comma alone.

(e) A parenthetic expression SHALL take two commas or none.

(f) A colon SHALL follow an independent clause, and SHALL NOT split a verb from its object.

(g) An exclamation mark SHALL be kept for a true exclamation, never used for emphasis.

(h) Quotes SHALL be straight.

(i) An arrow SHALL be `->`.

## § 9. Usage

(a) A formal document SHALL NOT use contractions.

(b) Numerals SHALL be used for numbers not less than 10, and for units of measurement, time,
and money.

(c) A number that opens a sentence SHALL be spelled out.

(d) One space SHALL follow a colon or a period.

(e) Dates SHALL read 2026-11-05.

(f) Where this standard is silent, the U.S. Government Publishing Office Style Manual
governs.

Notes: Taken from DoDM 5110.04 volume 1 para 6.4; (f) is its para 6.1, and the library holds the
2016 edition of the Style Manual. Two departures: (d), where para 6.4.c(6) sets two spaces, and
(e), where para 6.4.e sets "month day, year", because these dates sort and are unambiguous.

## § 10. Data

(a) Exhibits SHALL follow `data-display.md`: the data carries the page.

## § 11. Technical writing

(a) Procedures, descriptions, notes, and warnings SHALL follow `technical-writing.md`.

requirement it enforces, each with a planted positive and a clean negative in `--selftest`;

is claimed.
